# Learning design and evidence

Lamplight is a Scripture memorization aid, not a validated educational
intervention, theology curriculum, clinical tool, or substitute for communal
study. Its design applies well-supported general learning principles
conservatively while keeping claims proportional to the evidence.

## 1. Retrieval rather than recognition alone

Daily Review asks a learner to attempt recall before revealing the passage.
This is retrieval practice: the act of bringing information to mind is itself
a learning event, not only a test of what is already known. Laboratory work
with prose passages found stronger delayed retention after retrieval practice
than after additional study, even when additional study produced greater
short-term confidence.

Lamplight therefore places the recall attempt before the answer and does not
award mastery merely for browsing, rereading, searching, or playing a
recognition-heavy game.

## 2. Spaced practice

Successful Daily Reviews schedule a passage across expanding calendar-day
intervals: **1, 2, 4, 7, 14, and 30 days**. A missed passage returns to a nearer
review rather than being treated as a failure.

Distributed practice is supported by a large literature, but no interval
sequence is universally optimal. The best spacing depends on the intended
retention period, material, learner, and prior performance. Lamplight's
sequence is a transparent, usable heuristic—not a scientifically proven
optimum for Scripture or for every learner. Future versions may evaluate an
adaptive scheduler, but should preserve understandable due dates and protect
existing progress during migration.

## 3. Feedback and honest errors

After an attempted Daily Review, Lamplight reveals the source text so the
learner can compare it with the attempted recall before self-grading. Practice
games expose correct content and count slips instead of hiding errors behind a
perfect score. Research indicates that feedback can correct both memory errors
and mistaken confidence.

Self-grading is intentionally low friction, but it is not objective
certification. A learner can grade generously or accidentally. The interface
should use nonjudgmental language, make “Still learning” easy to choose, and
offer a brief undo for mistaken taps.

## 4. Helpful difficulty, not punishment

Recall that requires effort can support durable learning, but difficulty is
useful only when it remains achievable and feedback is available. Lamplight
therefore uses graduated support—such as first-letter hints, word banks, and
reconstruction—rather than equating frustration with effectiveness.

Hints should be available without shame. A hint reduces the strength of the
evidence that unaided recall succeeded, so it may affect the learner's grade,
but it should never remove earned progress, demand payment, or create a
punitive failure loop.

## 5. Whole passages and meaning

The default study unit is a whole passage. This preserves sequence and context
better than presenting every verse as an unrelated atom. Learners may still
create individual-verse units when that serves their purpose.

Verbatim recall is not the same as interpretation, application, historical
understanding, or spiritual formation. Lamplight should keep references and
context visible and encourage reading surrounding chapters. Topic labels are
navigation aids, not doctrinal judgments, and require human theological and
editorial review.

## 6. Why games do not certify mastery

Flash, Fill the Blank, Passage Builder, and Verse Match provide varied practice
and engagement. They differ in cue strength and can make another retrieval
attempt more inviting. They also permit guessing, recognition, partial cues,
or repeated exposure.

For that reason:

- Games may award the separate oil currency and report slips.
- Games do not advance spaced-review levels or due dates.
- Game completion is not called mastery.
- Daily Review is the only workflow that changes mastery state.
- A streak should represent the precisely defined daily habit shown in the UI,
  not imply retention or spiritual standing.

## 7. Motivation and learner welfare

Oil, flames, gentle animation, and streaks are intended to make returning feel
welcoming. They must remain informational and noncommercial: no ads, paid
streak repair, loot boxes, manipulative countdowns, or loss-aversion prompts.
Reduced-motion preferences, keyboard operation, readable contrast, clear
errors, and reset/export controls are part of pedagogy because learners cannot
practice effectively through an inaccessible or untrustworthy interface.

## 8. What Lamplight may and may not claim

Reasonable claim: Lamplight **uses** retrieval practice, distributed review,
answer feedback, and progressively reduced cueing—techniques supported across
many learning contexts.

Unsupported claims include:

- that Lamplight is the “best” or uniquely effective memory method;
- that its exact interval schedule is optimal;
- that completing a game proves long-term retention;
- that it improves comprehension, faith, mental health, or academic outcomes;
- that the current app has been validated for children, older adults,
  disabilities, or cognitive impairment; or
- that all learners should memorize the same translation or passages.

Any future effectiveness claim should specify the outcome, population,
comparison, retention interval, sample size, preregistered method, and limits.
